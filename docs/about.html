🌊 What is ABYSS?

ABYSS (Acinetobacter baumannii Yielding Surveillance System) is a comprehensive genomic resource that integrates typing, resistance, virulence, and epidemiological metadata for publicly available Acinetobacter baumannii genomes into a single, queryable, user-friendly interface.

A. baumannii is the WHO's priority-1 critical pathogen for new antibiotic development, the archetypal cause of ventilator-associated pneumonia, and one of the few organisms on Earth that can survive for weeks on a dry hospital surface. Existing resources present fragments of the surveillance picture — a typing database here, a resistance catalogue there, an ICU outbreak report somewhere else. ABYSS brings them together.

🌊 The name
abyss (n.) — an immeasurably deep gulf or great space.

Acinetobacter baumannii lives in the abyss. Not the deep ocean — the deep hospital. The ventilator tubing. The mattress rail. The dry surface at 3am that nobody swabbed. The gap between equipment that infection control has stopped looking at.

These are the overlooked reservoirs of the ICU — the small, dark spaces where carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii survives for weeks, colonises the next patient, and waits. Surveillance fails in exactly those places, because we tend to look where we've always looked.

ABYSS descends into those spaces. From the genomes already sequenced and already public, the resource yields the surveillance that routine ICU rounds miss — every respiratory isolate, every bloodstream infection, every catheter tip, every dry-surface swab. It is a watch kept over the parts of the hospital nobody thinks to watch.

— Named in the ARGUS tradition: a watchful figure, a hundred eyes, a night that never sleeps.

🎯 Why ABYSS?

Acinetobacter baumannii is one of the three or four organisms that most directly determine whether an ICU can safely treat its sickest patients. It is:

  • The WHO priority-1 critical pathogen for new antibiotic development
  • A leading cause of ventilator-associated pneumonia and ICU bloodstream infections
  • Capable of surviving weeks on dry surfaces — the reason ICU outbreaks close wards
  • An organism for which pan-drug-resistant strains already exist

Global genomic surveillance is essential for tracking emergent clones, understanding carbapenem-resistance mechanisms, and informing infection control. The data exists. What hasn't existed is a way to actually use it without weeks of setup.

🔒 The retrieval bottleneck

A typical surveillance question — "give me all IC2 isolates from respiratory sources in West Africa that carry OXA-23" — requires, in the current ecosystem, that you either:

  • Download and re-annotate the entire repository locally (weeks of compute)
  • Write custom scripts to query static TSV archives (breaks with every update)
  • Manually navigate multiple standalone tools (hours per query, doesn't scale)

None of these works for a researcher in a low-resource setting with a laptop. None works for a clinical microbiologist who needs an answer this afternoon. And none produces the one thing every downstream tool actually consumes: a clean accession list.

ARGUS

Antimicrobial Resistance Genomic Unified Surveillance

ABYSS is the third resource in ARGUS — a family of independent, open genomic surveillance sites, each dedicated to one of the WHO priority ESKAPE pathogens. Every resource shares the same architecture: accession-first, metadata-integrated, no login, no install, MIT-licensed. Each has its own name, its own DOI, and its own community.

Named for Argus Panoptes — the hundred-eyed giant of Greek myth who never slept — the umbrella is a bet that AMR surveillance has to be unified to be useful, and that the pathogens driving the crisis deserve the same treatment.

🗺️ Roadmap

ABYSS is built incrementally toward a single milestone: complete typing annotation of every public A. baumannii genome in the NCBI Assembly database.

  • v0.1 — Metadata foundation LIVE
    59,160 accessions indexed with country, source, host, and year from NCBI BioSample. GCA↔GCF deduplication via numeric stem. Geography page live.
  • v0.2 — Full typing layer LIVE
    48,038 genomes typed through AcinetoScope and the Shelenkov 2024 dataset — Pasteur + Oxford MLST, IC1–IC12 assignment, Kaptive K-locus capsule, AMRFinderPlus (carbapenemase-aware), VFDB, PlasmidFinder. All 12 pages live: MLST, Capsule, Master, AMR, Virulence, Plasmid, Statistics, Geography, Lineages, Downloads.
  • v0.3 — Full public collection IN PROGRESS
    Extend typing to the remaining NCBI Assembly collection — both GCF (RefSeq) and GCA (GenBank) genomes — to reach the full ~59,000 accession base indexed in v0.1.
  • v1.0 — Full resource
    All public genomes typed · citable Zenodo DOI · frozen, versioned release · peer-reviewed methods manuscript.

📦 Data sources

ABYSS combines three publicly available resources into a single integrated view:

1. Genome assemblies — NCBI Assembly

Every genome in ABYSS is sourced from the NCBI Assembly database. Accessions are the primary key. Where both a GenBank (GCA_) and RefSeq (GCF_) version exist, the RefSeq version is preferred; assemblies with no RefSeq counterpart are kept as GenBank.

2. Epidemiological metadata — NCBI BioSample

Country of origin, isolation source, host species, and collection year were extracted from NCBI BioSample records attached to each assembly. Source values were normalised into broad clinical, environmental, food, and animal categories while preserving the raw submitter strings for provenance. Coverage: 96% country, 83% source, 91% host, 95% year.

3. Typing annotations — generated by AcinetoScope

MLST (Oxford and Pasteur schemes) and K-locus capsule annotations are produced with AcinetoScope, the species-optimized typing pipeline published in Scientific Reports (2026).

4. Gene presence/absence — Shelenkov et al. (2024)

Antimicrobial resistance genes, virulence factors (VFDB), and plasmid replicons were sourced from the A. baumannii genomic epidemiology dataset published by Shelenkov, Mikhaylova & Akimkin in Data (MDPI), 2024. The original wide matrices were re-processed into long-format TSVs (accession × gene) to enable interactive retrieval in ABYSS.

⚙️ Methods & tools

All typing annotations in ABYSS are produced by AcinetoScope — an automated, comprehensive pipeline for genomic analysis of Acinetobacter baumannii. It integrates quality control, dual-scheme MLST, capsule typing, AMR detection, virulence and plasmid profiling, and critical-gene flagging into a single automated workflow. AcinetoScope wraps:

  • Quality control — FASTA validation with FastANI species confirmation against curated A. baumannii references
  • MLST — Multi-locus sequence typing, dual-scheme (Oxford: cpn60, fusA, gltA, pyrG, recA, rplB, rpoB; Pasteur) with IC1–IC12 assignment mlst (Seemann) + PubMLST
  • Capsule (K-locus) — K-type and OC-locus determination from the capsular polysaccharide biosynthesis locus Kaptive 3
  • AMR genes — Comprehensive resistance determinant detection AMRFinderPlus + ABRicate against CARD, ResFinder, NCBI, MEGARes 3.0, BacMet
  • Point mutations — Resistance-conferring point variants AMRFinderPlus
  • Virulence factors — VFDB gene screening ABRicate + VFDB
  • Plasmid profiling — Replicon detection using Acinetobacter-specific rep genes APT and PlasmidFinder
  • Critical gene flagging — Priority markers for infection control: carbapenemases, ESBLs, colistin and tigecycline resistance custom
  • Lineage catalogue — literature-curated International Clone reference custom

The AcinetoScope pipeline is open source and available on GitHub. The ABYSS website generator code is available at github.com/bbeckley-hub/abyss.
